### Closed forms (derived independently)
For one bag with scores s, relevant set P and irrelevant set N:
- **OneError.** Under a uniform order, the top label is uniform on T = argmax-set, so E = 1 − |T∩P|/|T|. Correct (`tie_neutral_metrics.py:51-52`).
- **Coverage.** `miml_metrics.py` gives (rank from top of the lowest-ranked relevant label) − 1. That label lies in the tie group at s* = min_P s. Above the group sit a labels. The r relevant labels occupy a uniform r-subset of the group's g slots, and the expected maximum of that subset is r(g+1)/(r+1). So E = a + r(g+1)/(r+1) − 1. Correct (`:62-66`).
- **RankingLoss.** Each tied (p, n) pair is reversed with probability ½, which gives (#{s_p<s_n} + ½#{s_p=s_n})/(|P||N|). A bag with no relevant or no irrelevant label scores 0 and stays in the mean, as in sklearn. Correct (`:74-80`).
- **AP.** Given that p sits at slot j of its g-group, its rank A + j is fixed. The other r−1 relevant labels of the group fill r−1 of the remaining g−1 slots uniformly, so the expected number ahead of p is (j−1)(r−1)/(g−1). This is the hypergeometric mean, and it can be taken inside because the denominator is fixed given j. Then average over j uniform on 1..g. At g = 1, `ahead = 0`. Correct (`:91-100`).

The primary implementation is correct, and the brute-force test pins it: every mutant of a primary closed form fails at least six tests.

## Findings

**1. MAJOR: the defect statement says the deposited rule is biased in one direction, and the amendment's own evidence contradicts that.** `AMENDMENT.md:16` says the deposited rule "would therefore have biased every ranking-metric comparison against tie-heavy baselines, in favour of the method under test". This holds for RankingLoss: sklearn charges a full error for each tied pair, so tie-neutral is never higher, and it is lower for all 7 KDIS methods. It also holds for Coverage on this fold, where all 7 improve. It is false for OneError and AP. In the evidence file, tie-neutral **worsens** OneError for MIMLkNN (0.2759→0.2847), MIMLBRkNN (0.1379→0.1724) and MIMLIBLR (0.7241→0.7908). It **lowers** AP for MIMLBRkNN (0.6954→0.6893) and MIMLBagging (0.4742→0.4554). Line 27 of the amendment says so itself for AP, so lines 16 and 27 contradict each other. A referee looking for an amendment that favours the authors will check this first. Line 16 also asserts without measurement that "ties barely affect" TabPFN/logreg. Degenerate labels predicted constant (§1.3 F4) and saturated logreg probabilities produce exact ties there too.
*Fix:* rewrite line 16 per metric. The deposited rule penalises tied outputs systematically on RankingLoss, and on OneError, Coverage and AP it makes the value depend on label index, in a direction that varies by method. Extend line 27 to all four metrics with the evidence-file numbers. Replace "barely" with a measured statement: for every arm, report the share of test bags with a tie that involves a relevant label, or state that it is reported with the results.

**2. MAJOR: the "what had been observed" disclosure is incomplete, and its final sentence overclaims.** `AMENDMENT.md:25` says "No method-versus-library difference had been computed under any rule" and "A1 therefore cannot have been shaped by an H1–H5 outcome". But the submitted manuscript compares MIML-PFN/logreg with KDIS (scored by Mulan's own convention, i.e. a tie rule) and with MIMLNN on the same datasets, and the plan itself tabulates those effects (§7 Part B, "Observed effects (descriptive, submitted protocol)"). Those method-versus-library differences were known when A1 was written. A1 also moves the primary rule from the deposited one toward the convention behind the submitted KDIS numbers: on RankingLoss, Mulan's rule is close to tie-neutral and far from the deposited one. In addition, the three-rule table for all 7 KDIS methods (evidence file) was in hand before the choice, and the alternatives considered (keep the deposited rule; adopt Mulan's rule) are not stated.
*Fix:* state that (i) submitted-protocol comparisons were known (§7 B), with the rule each used; (ii) the T03-b three-rule table for 7 methods on one fold was seen before the decision; (iii) the alternatives considered and why tie-neutral was chosen (it is label-order-free, and Mulan's rule is index-dependent too). Replace "cannot have been shaped by an H1–H5 outcome" with "no H1–H5 outcome under the revision protocol existed. The only method-versus-library comparisons known were the submitted-protocol ones in §7 B, under different scorers and splits."

**3. MAJOR: sensitivity row (a) is not "the deposited rule", and the rows are underspecified.** `AMENDMENT.md:22` calls row (a) "the deposited rule" but forbids re-selection. Under the deposited plan, inner selection (§1.3) would itself have used the deposited scorer. Row (a) is therefore a hybrid: candidates chosen by the tie-neutral rule, scored by the deposited one. It does not answer the question a skeptical reader asks of an outcome-dependent amendment: what would the deposited plan have concluded? Selection differs where it matters. Under the deposited rule MIMLkNN can hardly win on RankingLoss; under A1 it can. Re-selection is cheap, because every inner prediction is stored. Only a changed winner needs an extra refit, or `--refit all`. The rows also leave open: (i) whether the §1.6 test, Holm and the global Wilcoxon are re-run on each sensitivity row, or only means are shown; (ii) that each row is applied to **both** sides of every contrast (Mulan conventions for TabPFN/logreg/H5 as well); (iii) that the rows decide nothing.
*Fix:* define (a) as the full deposited pipeline, i.e. re-selection on the stored inner predictions plus the deposited outer scoring. Where the re-selected winner differs, refit it, or at minimum report "selection differs: <candidate>". Keep the current hybrid only as an extra row, labelled as such. State (i)–(iii) explicitly: for example, "each sensitivity row reports the §1.6 statistic, unadjusted p and Holm within the same family. It is descriptive and changes no verdict."

**4. MAJOR: binding plan text is superseded without being named.** A1's "Deposited rule" list (`AMENDMENT.md:11`) names §1.3 and §1.5 bullet 2 only. It omits:
- §6.8 T03-b (`PREREGISTERED.md:563`): "**Primary scoring stays `miml_metrics.py` on the dumped predictions**". This is an explicit definition of primary scoring that A1 overrides. Item 5 covers the checks but not this sentence.
- The §1.5 fallback (`PREREGISTERED.md:157`). If a harness cannot dump per-bag scores, its native scoring becomes primary, gated by T03-f agreement **with `miml_metrics.py`**. Native scoring cannot be tie-neutral, so the fallback and A1 conflict. T03 shows the fallback is not triggered (PredictDump and the Octave driver dump scores), but the rule must say what happens.
- §1.8 (`PREREGISTERED.md:224`): "if any later local text differs from it, the archived copy governs". A1 is valid only once deposited (see finding 5).
*Fix:* add these three to "Deposited rule". Add one sentence: "§1.5 fallback: not triggered (T03-b/c/f). If it ever were, the affected harness is reported on its native rule, as a separate comparator, never mixed with tie-neutral scores."

**5. MAJOR: nothing enforces "deposited before any run they govern".** `AMENDMENT.md:3` and DECISIONS ("to be deposited as Zenodo version v1.1 before the first one") promise this. Yet the real-data guard (`deposit_check.deposit_recorded`) checks only plan line 8, which is already open, and the harness already scores under A1. A production run today would produce primary numbers under a rule that §1.8 says does not yet govern. The project has used mechanical guards for exactly this kind of ordering (T03-a gate, deposit guard).
*Fix:* add a close-only guard. The runners (`run_nested_library.py`, `run_tabpfn_arms.py`, H2/H5 CLIs) refuse real data unless `AMENDMENT.md` A1 carries a `Deposited:` line with the v1.1 DOI and date, in the same grammar as `deposit_check`. Test it on a synthetic file. Alternatively, record in DECISIONS that no real run starts before v1.1 and add the check to the C-queue start. The guard is preferred.

## Summary
The mathematics and the code of the primary rule are correct. I derived the closed forms independently, they agree with exhaustive enumeration to 1.1e-14, and every primary mutant is killed. The Mulan sensitivity reimplementation is also correct: it matches weka's first-element grouping and Mulan's bag conventions, with 0/3 002 mismatches against Java. Scoring that feeds selection and stored results is on the primary rule. What fails is the amendment text, which a referee will read as the record. It misstates the direction of the defect (finding 1). It understates what was known (finding 2). It defines a "deposited rule" sensitivity row that does not reproduce the deposited pipeline (finding 3). It leaves binding §6.8, §1.5 fallback and §1.8 text unnamed (finding 4). And it has no mechanical guard for its own deposit-before-run promise (finding 5). The minor findings are test gaps and wording.

VERDICT: NOT RESOLVED — 12 findings
